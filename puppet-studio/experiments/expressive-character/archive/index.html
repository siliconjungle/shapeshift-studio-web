The construction

Draw the target. Animate the puppet.

1. Separate the useful pieces

Rigid armour, deformable cloth, continuous face surface, facial details and a few hair locks.

2. Bind one coherent rig

Shared anchors, vector points and small deformation cages keep cheeks, ink and markings together.

3. Animate controls continuously

Jaw, cheeks, gaze, brows, spine and hands move along coordinated curves with different timing.

4. Draw the exceptional moments

Aligned extreme poses, perspective changes and one-frame smears supplement the moving rig.

Most motion is deformation

A smile lifts the cheeks and lower lids while the mouth corners travel outward. The jaw opens independently. The face and its marking share the same deformation.

Body movement uses the existing hierarchy, contacts and soft bindings. Armour follows rigid pivots; the scarf and hair get delayed bends.

Some shapes need a new drawing

A tall panic jaw or profile needs a purpose-drawn shape. Both versions share neck, jaw, eye and mouth anchors. A fast change can pass through a blink or smear.

A slow slider needs compatible intermediate shapes. We cannot smoothly morph unrelated automatically traced images just because they are SVGs.

Inspected in the real game

The Little Gods foundation

The game already separates heads, bodies, arms, hands and boots. Its curved arm strips deform the vector art through a small GPU control cage. God portraits use ordinary Puppet Studio projects with separate head, body, eyes and halo.

Actual existing game assets shown for construction reference. The richer face rig and speech system above still need to be authored and integrated.

Talking is a coordinated face performance

Mouth shapes follow timestamped sounds. The jaw, cheeks and lip corners interpolate; M/B/P keep their crisp closure. Gaze, brows and gestures have a separate acting track.

The mouth concept sheets need mechanical cleanup, particularly L and F/V contacts. They are guides for editable paths, not final lip-sync assets.
