How they become puppets

One shared foundation. Different kinds of acting.

Frog: squash, stretch and inflation

A continuous face/throat surface carries cheek and jaw deformation. Eyes, pupils, lids, mouth interior and tongue are separately controlled and clipped.

The hat has a brim, cone and floppy tip. The scarf follows the throat, while the tip and ties react later. Hands have explicit cheek/chin contacts.

Wood: hinges, plates and loose ornaments

The upper mask, lower jaw, eye/brow shapes and cheek pieces form an articulated face. The jaw opens on shared hinges; individual wooden pieces retain their solidity.

Straw, cords and beads trail behind the head. Special expressions use authored mask shapes with matching anchors.

What makes it smoothly animated?

1. Registered SVG pieces

Generate, reduce colours, trace, clean up and align shared eye, jaw, neck and mouth anchors.

2. Continuous controls

Interpolate meaningful face points and small deformation cages. Ink and fill follow the same surface.

3. Coordinated timing

Eyes lead, head follows, shoulders react, then the hat or beads settle. Hand contacts stay attached.

4. Purpose-drawn extremes

Compatible corrective poses and aligned replacements handle topology changes; short smears bridge fast accents.

A slow transition needs matching intermediate geometry. A pile of independently generated expression pictures will not morph cleanly by itself. The actual portrait demo will animate the rig between targets, with occasional deliberate drawing changes.

Built on the existing game tools

I inspected the actual Little Gods SVG pieces and code: villagers use separate head/body/limb artwork, curved arm strips and a small GPU control cage. God portraits are normal editable Puppet Studio projects with separate heads, bodies and eyes.

We can reuse that foundation alongside Studio’s vector point poses, corrective layers, soft bindings, contacts and follow-through. The richer facial controls, shared anchors, eye/mouth clipping and audio-timed speech player still need to be wired together.

Scope is portraits and optional hands. Full-body movement is outside this first demo.

First animated proof

Rest → smile → suppressed emotion → extreme reaction → recovery, plus one spoken line and one hand-to-face contact. Test slow scrubbing and interruption as well as normal playback.

Keep the artwork stable and inexpensive

Parse, triangulate and bind once. Update controls during playback. Cache discrete corrected drawings; avoid full-scene outline passes and per-frame tracing.
